Why No Gyan?

Experience gives you confidence in what you know. Adventure requires you to walk toward what you don’t.

I’m starting a series called No Gyan.

Gyan means wisdom or knowledge. In India, “giving gyan” can also mean dispensing advice—often whether anyone asked for it or not.

So why No Gyan?

Because I don’t have the answers.

I’ve accumulated some scar tissue, pattern recognition, and beliefs over the years. But I’m also starting a new adventure where much of what lies ahead is unknown.

That’s the paradox.

Experience gives you confidence in what you know. Adventure requires you to walk toward what you don’t.

And I’m realizing that fear often lives in that gap.

You can’t eliminate the unknowns. If you could, it probably wouldn’t be much of an adventure.

But you can name them. Examine them. Talk about them. Turn unknown unknowns into known unknowns.

And somehow, knowing what you don’t know makes it a little less intimidating to move forward anyway.

That’s what No Gyan will be: things I’m learning, unlearning, questioning—and sometimes getting wrong—in public.

Maybe there’ll be some gyan in there after all.

And even when there isn’t, maybe there’ll be a little less fear.

It’s free. Take it or leave it.
